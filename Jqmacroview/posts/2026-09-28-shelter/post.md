*Data through August 2026. Sources: Zillow mid-tier home values and rents, BLS CPI via FRED.*

## The short version

- Market rents lead CPI shelter inflation by **13 months**. Home values lead by **17 months**.
- A simple model built on that lead explains about **72%** of the movement in shelter inflation since 2016. It projects shelter inflation of about **3.1% now, a shallow low of about 3.0% around April 2027, and about 3.2% by September 2027.**
- Most of the disinflation from shelter is already behind us. What's left is a plateau, not a further decline.
- Pass-through speed varies by region: about **11 months in the West, 13 in the South, and 15 in the Northeast and Midwest.**

## 1. Rents lead CPI by about a year

Shelter is the largest single component of CPI, and it moves slowly. The official number reflects rent paid by all tenants, most of whom are mid-lease. Zillow tracks asking rents on new leases, which is where changes show up first.

To measure the lag, I compared Zillow's year-over-year rent growth with CPI inflation at every lead from 0 to 24 months. All three rent measures peak at 13 months. The fit is tightest for CPI rent of primary residence (about 0.91), then owners' equivalent rent and total shelter (about 0.85). Home values peak later, at 17 months (about 0.78), because price changes pass through to rents before they reach CPI.

The takeaway: rent growth today is a good read on shelter inflation about a year from now.

## 2. The market today: rents have settled, and the country is split

Rent growth has held steady near 2.5–2.9% for a year, while home values and rents diverge sharply by region.

**Nationally.** Home value growth briefly turned negative in 2023, rebounded to about 5% in 2024, then slowed to about 0.5% in late 2025. It has since recovered to about 1.7%. Rent growth has been far steadier — slightly below the 3–4% pace of the years before the pandemic.

**By state.** Home prices vary enormously by state, so I compared growth rates rather than dollar amounts.

- **Sun Belt and Mountain West: soft.** Home values are down year over year in Texas (-1.4%), Florida (-1.2%), Colorado (-1.0%), Arizona (-0.9%), Nevada (-0.9%), Washington (-0.5%) and Kentucky (-0.4%). Rent growth is weakest in Colorado (+0.7%), Texas (+0.8%), Arizona (+1.0%) and Florida (+1.1%).
- **Midwest and Northeast: firm.** Home values are up about 5% in Illinois, Wisconsin, Connecticut and New York. Rents are up 4.5–5% in Illinois, Iowa, New Jersey, Wisconsin and South Dakota. Hawaii has the fastest rent growth (+5.3%).
- **Rents are outpacing home values in 42 of 50 states.** The median state has rents up 3.2% and home values up 2.1%.

## 3. The projection: a plateau near 3%

The model projects shelter inflation holding between 3.0% and 3.2% through September 2027.

> **Model:** Shelter YoY (t + 13 months) = 1.93 + 0.43 × Zillow rent YoY (t) · R² = 0.72

- **The catch-up is done.** At about 3.1% today, actual shelter inflation now sits right where the model projects, so there is little room left to fall.
- **The low point is shallow and near.** Rent growth hit its low in March 2026, at 2.5%, which maps to a shelter low around April 2027. After that, this spring's rent rebound (yearly growth up from 2.5% to 2.9% since March) pushes shelter inflation gently back up.

## 4. Regional timing: the West feels it first

| Region | Rent → shelter lead |
|---|---|
| West | 11 months |
| South | 13 months |
| Northeast | 15 months |
| Midwest | 15 months |

The soft markets and the firm markets are on different clocks. Sun Belt and Mountain West weakness passes through faster, so it should show up in CPI for the West and South first. Midwest and Northeast firmness reaches CPI more slowly, keeping those regions' shelter inflation elevated into late 2027.

## My view

For two years, falling shelter inflation did a lot of work pulling core inflation down, as CPI caught up to a rent slowdown that had already happened in the market. That catch-up is now complete.

Market rents imply shelter inflation holding at about 3% through 2027. That's roughly its pre-pandemic pace of about 3.3%, but no longer falling. For the inflation outlook, shelter shifts from a tailwind to a neutral-to-slightly-firm factor, so getting core inflation lower from here will need help from other categories.

The data to watch is monthly rent readings this winter. If they hold up better than last winter's dip, the path turns higher sooner than the model projects.
