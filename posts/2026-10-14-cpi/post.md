Core CPI rose 0.3% in September, while headline hit 0.6% as gasoline jumped 9.1%. Core services added 0.31pp to the monthly core print, and shelter (+0.4%, up from +0.3%) accounted for 0.18pp of that. Core goods added just 0.07pp.

What changed is the swing factor. In August, education and communication added 0.11pp on a 5.4% jump in telephone services; in September it added nothing as telephone prices slipped 0.1% and internet services fell 1.3%. Car rental (+5.4%) and airline-led public transportation (+1.2%) were small positives within transportation services.

Zoom out and the trend is steadier than the monthly noise suggests. Core CPI has averaged 0.23% a month in 2026, against 0.26% in the same months of 2024–25, a gap too small to be statistically meaningful.

## Seasonality: a January firmness and a mid-year dip, little else

The CPI breakdowns are already seasonally adjusted, so any calendar pattern that remains is residual seasonality. The sample (August 2023 to September 2026) gives only two to four readings per calendar month, so treat what follows as indicative. Two patterns recur:

- **January is firm.** Core CPI runs 0.11pp above its own average in January and core services 0.18pp. Transportation services is +0.93pp, with motor vehicle insurance +0.57pp and public transportation +1.18pp.
- **May–June is soft.** Core CPI is 0.12–0.15pp below average, rent of shelter −0.15pp in June, and insurance and public transportation are weak too.
- Water, sewer and trash runs firm in January–February, new vehicles soft in May–June and recreation commodities firm in January and June, but the pattern is not strong or consistent enough to lean on.

**Goods tell the pipeline story.** PPI goods less food and energy average +0.45% a month (z = 2.4), against +0.05% for CPI core goods. Producer costs are rising much faster than retail prices, which points to margin compression or a lagged pass-through to come.

**Energy is a separate factor.** PPI energy has averaged +2.8% a month this year, against −0.3% in 2024–25. Core CPI leaves energy out, so it helps explain the gap with the PPI headline but not the gap in core.

PPI foods are flat (−0.03%) and construction is firm at +0.46%, both with no direct core CPI equivalent.

## Bottom line

Core inflation is running slightly below its 2024–25 pace (0.23% a month against 0.26%), with insurance the main drag and shelter a touch softer on average. Producer prices tell a hotter story: PPI goods are averaging +0.45% a month against +0.05% for core goods CPI, and freight is the driver of PPI transport. Services are broadly in line across the two indices. If producers stop absorbing those costs, goods CPI is where the pressure shows up first, and seasonality does not change that read.

## What to watch

- **September PPI.** It is not out yet, so PPI figures run through August. The key test is whether freight and goods PPI stay hot or the August +2.3% transport jump reverses.
- **Pass-through.** If producer goods keep running well above CPI goods, core goods CPI is the place to look for a catch-up.
- **Insurance.** Motor vehicle and health insurance are the main disinflation in core services; a turn there would matter more than shelter.
